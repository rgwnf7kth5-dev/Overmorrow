## Facts
- Preliminary seat allocation (Electoral Commission data via RNZ): National 42 (42 electorate, 0 list), Labour 36 (21+15), Greens 16 (2+14), ACT 13 (1+12), NZ First 12 (0+12), Opportunity 7 (0+7), Te Pāti Māori 5 (5+0) = 131 seats — https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/election-2026-results (read 2026-11-25T17:43:57Z) "National : 42 electorate seats, 0 list seats, 42 total seats". (RNZ's election-night story said Labour 35 at 98.5% counted; the full preliminary count gives 36.)
- Right bloc 67 of 131; 66 needed — 1News 8 Nov: "On preliminary results, the coalition has 67 seats in a projected 131-seat House, one more than the 66 needed to govern." — https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/11/08/fewer-than-500-votes-in-it-the-electorates-still-too-close-to-call/ (published 2026-11-08T01:41Z, read 2026-11-25T17:44:42Z). interest.co.nz: left bloc Labour+Greens+TPM 57; with Opportunity 64 — https://www.interest.co.nz/public-policy/141068/three-weeks-waiting-how-specials-seat-race-and-overhang-could-reshape-next (published 2026-11-15 06:00 NZDT, read 2026-11-25T17:44:42Z). (Latin Times of 8 Nov says 68 of 131 citing RNZ; RNZ's own table sums to 67 — treated as an error.)
- Party vote, preliminary: Labour 710,330 (29.36%), National 706,680 (29.21%) — https://www.latintimes.com/new-zealand-election-special-votes-majority-601038 (published 2026-11-08T16:53-05:00, read 2026-11-25T17:44:17Z). RNZ election night: Greens 13.3%, Opportunity 6%, ACT 10.9%, NZ First 9.8% — https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics_election-2026/2420113/too-close-to-call-wait-begins-for-special-votes-after-cliffhanger-election-night (published 2026-11-08T06:54+13:00, read 2026-11-25T17:43:53Z).
- Overhang: National 7, Te Pāti Māori 4 on preliminary numbers (RNZ, 24 Nov) — https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/2760002/act-eyes-referendum-on-abolishing-maori-seats-in-coalition-talks (published 2026-11-24T12:41+13:00, read 2026-11-25T17:44:11Z) "On preliminary numbers this election National won seven overhang seats … and Te Pāti Māori won four." (AFP/Malay Mail 8 Nov had National 6, TPM 5; Wikipedia: 11 overhang seats in total.)
- Mechanics: National has no list seats, so its seat total moves only with electorates; "every electorate National wins or loses changes the size of Parliament" (1News 8 Nov, above). AFP: "National's overhang insulates it from any party voting changes." — https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2026/11/08/new-zealand-has-voted-but-who-won-why-the-election-is-still-too-close-to-call/242439 (published 2026-11-08, read 2026-11-25T17:44:17Z). Hence the right bloc's majority can be lost through ACT/NZ First list seats moving left on the party vote, or through National electorates flipping (each flip removes one bloc seat and one House seat).
- Desk arithmetic from those figures (not a source claim): one ACT/NZF list seat lost → 66/131 (majority kept); one National electorate flip → 66/130 (kept; interest.co.nz: Wairarapa flip "would bring National's seat total down to 41 and the overhang down to 130"); one list seat lost plus one National electorate flip → 65/130 (tie, NO); two list seats lost → 65/131 (NO). A TPM electorate lost to a non-overhang party shrinks the House without touching the bloc. 1News 25 Nov: "If Parliament loses one seat … and it's a 130-seat House, then 66 is a majority and a 65-65 split becomes possible."
- Special votes: about 399,000 outstanding (1News 8 Nov: "nearly 97,000 of them overseas and dictation votes"); ~14% of the total vote (interest.co.nz). RNZ 26 Nov: Commission statements "indicate only approximately 400,000 special votes were received this year", against 602,488 in 2023 and a 700,000 forecast; "lowest special vote total since 2014". Prof. Richard Shaw expects a higher rejection rate after the enrolment-deadline change (RNZ explainer above).
- Past shifts (RNZ explainer, above): "In 2017, 2020 and 2023, National lost two seats with special votes, while Labour gained one in 2017 and 2020, Te Pāti Māori gained one in 2020 and two in 2023, and the Greens gained one party seat in 2017 and 2023." interest.co.nz: "most of the previous elections since 2008 have seen one or two seats knocked off National once special votes are counted, with the left picking those up." 2005: National's election-night 49 fell to 48 after specials (Wikipedia, 2005 NZ general election, read 2026-11-25T17:47Z).
- Knife-edge electorates (preliminary margins; 1News 8 Nov and 25 Nov, interest.co.nz 15 Nov): Wairarapa — National's Mike Butterick leads Labour's Kieran McAnulty by 139 (17,943 v 17,804); Wellington Bays — Labour's Craig Renney leads Green Julie Anne Genter by 140 (left-on-left, no bloc effect); Mt Roskill — Labour's Michael Wood leads National's Carlos Cheung by 262 (a Cheung win would add a National seat and a House seat); Te Tai Tokerau — TPM's Aperahama Edwards leads Green Hūhana Lyndon by 436 (moves TPM overhang / House size); Hamilton East — National's Ryan Hamilton leads Labour's Georgie Dansey by 456; Hutt South — Labour's Ginny Andersen leads National's Chris Bishop by 889. In 2023 four electorates changed hands after election night, including Nelson (won by 26/29 votes).
- Coalition talks: formal talks wait for the official count (AFP via Malay Mail: "Coalition talks will commence once the final results are released on November 27"). Luxon is caretaker PM; National, ACT and NZ First "have said that they will work with each other again" (AFP). Luxon met Opportunity's Qiulae Wong; Wong says she negotiates first with the largest party (interest.co.nz, 1News 25 Nov). Luxon ruled out working with Opportunity during the campaign (1News 25 Nov). Disputes already: Seymour says Luxon's "finance stays with National" is "no longer tenable" (interest.co.nz); ACT floats a referendum on abolishing Māori seats in talks (RNZ 24 Nov).
- After the declaration: candidates have three working days to apply for judicial recounts; each takes 3–5 days; in 2023 three recounts followed (1News 24 Nov). Writ return deadline 15 Jan 2027 (Wikipedia).

# eu-uk-youth-21
- **Claim:** At the EU–UK summit in Brussels on Friday 20 November 2026, the EU and the UK announce that negotiations on a youth experience (youth mobility) scheme are concluded — i.e. the scheme is agreed, with or without a cap on numbers.
- **Criterion:** YES if, by 23:59 Brussels time (CET) on 20 Nov 2026, the summit's joint statement or an official statement of the European Council, the European Commission or the UK government says that negotiations on the youth experience / youth mobility scheme have been concluded, or that the scheme has been agreed (including "agreed in principle" with its main terms such as age range and length of stay), or that an agreement on it was initialled or signed. NO if the text only commits to negotiate or to conclude the scheme later (e.g. "by 2028"), or if the summit does not take place on 20 Nov. Tuition fees (handled in a parallel agreement) do not count either way. If no official text appears, reporting by two of Reuters, AFP, BBC, Guardian, Euronews, Politico decides.

The total number of participants has not been decided yet, although sources say it is likely to be around 100,000." — https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/18/eu-uk-summit-leaked-draft-reveals-ambition-and-limits-of-brexit-reset (published 2026-11-18T13:23+01:00, read 2026-11-18T17:53Z).

## Risks
- The two drafts differ: 3 Nov "welcomes the conclusion of negotiations" (YES wording) vs 17 Nov "aim to seal the agreement by 2028 at the latest" (could be read as negotiations still open, or as formal conclusion/ratification of an agreed scheme). The final text decides; a statement that "agrees the scheme's main parameters" while leaving signature to 2028 counts as YES under the criterion ("agreed in principle"), a pure commitment to negotiate is NO.
- Tuition fees and the cap remain open; the EU may hold back the youth scheme until the fees agreement is done.
- UK domestic politics: free-movement red line; Reform UK and Conservatives attack the deal.

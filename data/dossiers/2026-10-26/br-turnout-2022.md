# br-turnout-2022

- **Claim:** Turnout in the 25 Oct 2026 runoff is higher than the 79.41% of the 2022 runoff, per TSE.
- **Criterion:** YES if the TSE runoff turnout (comparecimento, % of the electorate) at 100% totalised, or the latest TSE figure at 26 Oct 2026 12:00 CET, is greater than 79.41%. NO if it is 79.41% or lower, or if the runoff is postponed or TSE publishes no figure by the deadline.
- **Deadline:** 2026-10-26T12:00:00+01:00
- **Written at:** 2026-10-23T19:05:00+02:00

## Base rate
- No compiled reference class in pipeline/reference-data for runoff vs first-round turnout. Sourced comparison: 2022 abstention fell from 20.95% (1st round) to 20.59% (runoff), i.e. turnout rose ~0.36 pp (https://www.cut.org.br/noticias/total-de-abstencoes-caiu-de-20-95-para-20-59-entre-o-1-e-o-2-turno-da-eleicao-1f2f; TSE 2022 page). n=1.
- Arithmetic: 2026 first round turnout 78.92%; YES needs a rise of more than 0.49 pp from the first round (2022's rise was 0.36 pp). The 2026 electorate is 158.7m vs 156.5m in 2022; 79.41% of 158,745,463 ≈ 126.06m voters.
- Note: dgabc reports 2022 first-round abstention variously as 20.95% and 20.79% (inconsistent within article).

## Risks
- Turnout counted as % of electorate; TSE's percentage reported to two decimals — near-tie at 79.41 resolves NO.
- A tight, polarised race could mobilise voters (as 2022) — or the record first-round abstention may persist. Runoffs often have only the presidential race and a few governor runoffs (6 units), less down-ballot pull.
- Turnout figure is published with totalisation; not an issue for the deadline given 2022 precedent.

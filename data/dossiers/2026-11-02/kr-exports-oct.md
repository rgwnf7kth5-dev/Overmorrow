  Earlier months were not collected (budget). Ratios are own arithmetic.
- **Working days (own calendar count, Customs convention: weekday = 1, Saturday = 0.5, public holidays = 0).** The count matches the published daily averages for 1–20 Oct (61.6/4.74 = 13.0), 1–20 Aug (55.2/3.94 = 14.0) and the July month (98.89/4.12 = 24.0). It does not exactly reproduce the 15.0 reported for 1–20 June (own count 14.5–15.5, depending on the 3 Jun local-election holiday), so allow ±0.5 day.
  - Oct 2026: holidays are Mon 5 Oct (substitute for National Foundation Day, Sat 3 Oct) and Fri 9 Oct (Hangul Day). That gives 1–20 Oct = 13 working days and 21–31 Oct = 9 (eight weekdays plus Saturdays 24 and 31). Full month 22.
  - Sep 2026: Chuseok is 24–26 Sep (Thu–Sat). That gives 1–20 Sep ≈ 15.5 and 21–30 Sep ≈ 6. Full month ≈ 21.5.
  - So October has about 0.5 more working days than September. October's first 20 days hold a smaller share of the month's working days (13/22 = 59%) than in Jun (≈67%), Aug (64%) and Sep (≈72%); July was similar at 60%.
- **Late-month daily rate vs first-20-days daily rate (own arithmetic, from the table and the day counts):** Jun ≈1.30 (5.36 vs 4.13); Jul ≈1.22 (4.63 vs 3.79); Aug ≈1.37 (5.38 vs 3.94); Sep ≈1.79 (8.25 vs 4.61, distorted by Chuseok bunching shipments into 21–23 and 28–30 Sep).
- **Arithmetic implied for October (own, not a forecast from any source):** full month = 61.6 + 9 × 4.74 × k.
  - k = 1.0: $104.3bn
  - k = 1.22: $113.6bn
  - k = 1.30: $117.1bn
  - k = 1.37: $120.0bn
  - k = 1.79: $138.0bn

  Applying the raw full/20-day ratios (1.648–1.801) instead gives $101.5–110.9bn. That method ignores October's back-loaded working days, so it is biased low.
- **Consensus:** no Reuters, Yonhap or Bloomberg poll for October 2026 was found. A search on 30 Oct returned only polls from 2019–2025. For September, the TE consensus was +61.7% y/y against an actual +83.5%, a beat of about 22 points — Trading Economics https://tradingeconomics.com/south-korea/exports-yoy/news/588400 (published 2026-10-01 00:11, read 2026-10-30T17:45Z) "surpassing estimates of 61.7%". In October 2025 the consensus was −0.6% against an actual +3.6% (ING, above). Surprises have been large and to the upside during the chip boom. No systematic surprise series was collected.
- Minister Kim Jung-kwan expects $1 trillion in annual exports in 2026 — Korea Times 1 Oct (above). That needs about $170bn over October–December after $830bn in January–September (own arithmetic), far below current run rates, so it gives no signal for October.

## For a number forecast
- Previous: Sep $120.9bn (record), Aug $98.25bn, Jul $98.89bn, Jun $102.2bn. Oct 2025: $59.57bn.
- Consensus: none found.
- Spread of the 20-day-based arithmetic: $104bn (flat daily rate) to $138bn (September-style surge). The k values from June to August cluster at 1.22–1.37, which gives $113.6–120.0bn.
- Upside surprise vs consensus in Sep 2026: about +22 pts y/y (TE).

## Risks
- Chip shipment timing: chips are 45% of exports, and month-end chip shipments drive the late-month surge (September's last six working days averaged $8.25bn a day).
- The day count is my own and carries about ±0.5 day of error. One extra or missing working day moves the estimate by about $5–6bn.
- The headline is the preliminary ministry figure; later revisions do not count.
- Release slip is negligible.

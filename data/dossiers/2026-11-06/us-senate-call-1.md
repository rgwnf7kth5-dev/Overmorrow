# us-senate-call-1
- **Claim:** AP declares between 00:00 ET on 4 November and 23:59 ET on 5 November 2026 that one party has won control of the US Senate.
- **Criterion:** YES if, between 00:00 ET on 4 Nov and 23:59 ET on 5 Nov 2026, the Associated Press declares that the Democratic or the Republican Party has won control of the Senate after the elections of 3 Nov 2026, or, if AP makes no explicit declaration, if AP race calls, added to the senators not up for election, bring Republicans to at least 50 seats or Democrats with independents who caucus with them to at least 51 in that time. A newly elected independent counts with the Democrats only if he or she has said publicly that he or she will caucus with them. AP's dated report decides; a call made in the window counts even if it is read later. Calls by other outlets do not count. NO if neither happens in the window, including if the election is not held. A declaration before 00:00 ET on 4 Nov falls outside the window; the morning run rewrites the claim in that case.
- **Deadline:** 2026-11-05T23:59:00-05:00 (05:59 CET on 6 Nov).
- **Written at:** 2026-11-03T18:15Z, before the first polls closed. No result is known. The morning run updates this.

- **Louisiana and Mississippi:** Louisiana now uses closed party primaries (Senate overview, above). Whether either state can need a general-election runoff was not established here.
- **Independents:** Dan Osborn (NE) and Seth Bodnar (MT) run as independents — us-senate-dem-1. A win leaves the caucus choice open unless stated.
- **Recount margin:** AP may decline a call under 0.5 points, and may still call a likely recount if the raw lead is too large to overturn — WBUR, above.

## Risks
- The call may come before 00:00 ET on 4 Nov, outside this window; the morning rewrites it.
- If Alaska is the tipping seat, no call is likely in the window.
- Several races within 3 pp; recount-range margins in a tipping state.
- An independent winner with no stated caucus choice can hold up the Democratic count.
- A call just after 23:59 ET resolves NO; AP's time stamp decides.

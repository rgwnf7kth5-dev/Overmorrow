## Facts
- DOJ filed the suit on 22 Oct 2026; the court noted "the United States initiated this action on October 22, 2026" — Alaska Public Media https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-27/state-of-alaska-says-doj-lawsuit-over-senate-race-has-no-legal-basis (published 2026-10-27 10:37 AKDT, read 2026-10-31T17:58Z).
- The suit seeks to block or delay certification if Sen. Dan S. Sullivan loses to Mary Peltola by less than the votes for Daniel J. Sullivan (Jr.), the Petersburg candidate whose name also appears on the ballot — Alaska Public Media (above); Newsweek https://newsweek.com/alaska-election-lawsuit-judge-fast-tracks-officials-defend-sullivan-ballots-12622694 (published 2026-10-27 10:46 UTC, read 2026-10-31T17:52Z).
- Intervention was granted on 28 Oct to Alaskans for Mary, the DSCC, the League of Women Voters of Alaska and Common Cause, after DOJ filed a "notice withdrawing opposition to motion to intervene"; DOJ filed a first amended complaint on 29 Oct — docket list, democracydocket.com (above).
- Gleason earlier set rulings on tight deadlines: "The Court intends to issue a ruling by 5:00 p.m. on October 28, 2026" (intervention) — Doc. 15 (above). She was reported as intending to rule on the motions to dismiss "by 5 p.m. on Thursday" (29 Oct) — Newsweek https://newsweek.com/alaska-senate-race-judge-ruling-doj-dan-sullivan-mary-peltola-victory-12628398 (published 2026-10-28 12:26 UTC, read 2026-10-31T17:55Z); the amended complaint then restarted the motions-to-dismiss briefing.
- Alaska Division of Elections (Doc. 72, 30 Oct) opposes expedited consideration: ballots "are comingled in the ballot box on Election Day", segregation would violate "the constitutional protections for ballot secrecy", the state "preserves them for twenty-two months after the election", and the US "waited until two weeks before Election Day to file a lawsuit about issues that arose months ago"; it asks that no response be due until the court rules on the motions to dismiss — https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2026-10-30-Alaska-election-boards-opposition-to-motion-for-expedited-consideration-of-motion-for-preliminary-injunction.pdf (filed 2026-10-30, read 2026-10-31T18:03Z).
- CBS: Woodward wrote "there is a substantial risk that Mr. Sullivan's sham candidacy will mislead voters" and "asked a judge to rule by Monday" — https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/2026-midterm-elections-campaigns-weekend-before/ (post 20:41 ET 30 Oct, read 2026-10-31T17:46Z).
- Related, not this case: in California, a 2–1 Ninth Circuit panel rejected a DOJ request to set aside disputed ballots ("there is no feasible way to identify and segregate the ballots of specific individuals without potentially catastrophic effects"), and Judge Trina Thompson issued a TRO against federal demands to separate out ballots — CBS (posts 15:43 and 22:54 ET 30 Oct). Gleason sits in the Ninth Circuit.
- Earlier history: state officials tried to remove Daniel J. Sullivan from the ballot in June; a state court restored him and the Alaska Supreme Court affirmed — SAN https://san.com/cc/trump-justice-department-escalate-fight-over-alaska-senate-ballot/ (published 2026-10-24 17:30 CDT, read 2026-10-31T17:57Z).

## Risks
- **Dismissal first:** a grant of the motions to dismiss moots the injunction request.
- **Partial grant ambiguity:** the motion is combined (discovery + preservation/segregation). An order granting only expedited discovery, or only a schedule, is "other relief" under the criterion (NO), but readers could argue it is a grant "in part" of the same motion. An order to "preserve" Sullivan-ranked ballots without segregating them is also ambiguous.
- **Timing:** a ruling on Sat 31 Oct (before the window) would resolve NO even if it grants segregation.
- **Appeal:** an emergency appeal to the Ninth Circuit after 2 Nov does not affect the claim.

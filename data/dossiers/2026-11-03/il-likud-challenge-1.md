# il-likud-challenge-1
- **Claim:** By 23:59 Israel time on 2 November 2026, Likud or one of its Knesset members files a formal legal challenge to the results of the Knesset election.
- **Criterion:** YES if between 00:00 Israel time on 1 Nov and 23:59 on 2 Nov 2026 Likud, or a Likud MK or candidate acting for it, files a formal petition, appeal or objection against the 2026 Knesset election results or their counting with the Central Elections Committee or the Supreme Court (High Court of Justice), as reported by the court or committee, ToI, Haaretz, Ynet or a wire. Announcements of intent, media calls for recounts and filings made before 1 Nov do not count. NO otherwise.

## Schedule
- **Election:** Tuesday 27 Oct 2026 — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Israeli_legislative_election (read 2026-10-31T17:48Z) "Official results ... scheduled to be confirmed on 4 November 2026".
- **Results:** the CEC published the final count overnight on 29–30 Oct. "The final official results will be submitted to President Isaac Herzog on November 4 following any examinations of errors or inquiries." The Knesset is to be sworn in on 10 Nov — Times of Israel liveblog https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-october-30-2026/ (entry 00:51 on 30 Oct, read 2026-10-31T17:55Z).
- **Legal deadline (Knesset Elections Law, s. 86, as summarised by JPost):** "Election appeals go to the Jerusalem District Court (administrative affairs) within 14 days of publication of results", with extension possible in special circumstances. A further appeal to the Supreme Court on a legal question needs leave from the court's president and must be filed within 30 days of judgment. An appeal does not suspend the Knesset — JPost https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-913187 (published 2026-10-29 09:29, updated 14:40, read 2026-10-31T18:02Z). Algemeiner says the same: "It has 14 days after the official results" — https://www.algemeiner.com/2026/10/30/netanyahus-only-61-runs-through-raam/ (published 2026-10-30 11:49, read 2026-10-31T17:59Z).
- **Implication (own reading):** the statutory election appeal lies with the Jerusalem District Court, which the criterion does not list. The 14-day clock starts at publication on about 4 Nov, so Likud has no legal pressure to file on 1–2 Nov. A pre-publication request to the CEC (e.g. to annul specific polling stations or examine errors) would fit the criterion, because the CEC is reviewing "examinations of errors or inquiries" until 4 Nov.

## Risks
- **Venue mismatch:** a filing with the Jerusalem District Court on 1–2 Nov, the statutory route, would not satisfy the criterion as written. The resolver may still count it, since it is the obvious "formal legal challenge".
- **Earlier filing:** if Likud already filed with the CEC on 29–31 Oct, a supplementary filing on 1–2 Nov might be reported as "Likud filed". It would then be hard to tell apart from the earlier one.
- **Who files:** a filing by a non-Likud party (Otzma, Shas) or by private voters does not count.
- **Political incentive:** Netanyahu may prefer coalition talks, for example with Ra'am or the Bennett defectors, to litigation before the mandate is assigned.

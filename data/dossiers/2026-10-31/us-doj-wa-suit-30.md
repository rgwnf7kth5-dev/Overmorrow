# us-doj-wa-suit-30
- **Claim:** On Thursday 29 or Friday 30 October 2026 (Pacific time), the US Justice Department goes to federal court against Washington state's election officials to have the ballots of about 400 alleged noncitizens set aside or kept from being counted.
- **Criterion:** YES if, between 2026-10-29T00:00:00-07:00 and 2026-10-30T23:59:59-07:00, the United States (Justice Department or a US Attorney's Office) files in any federal court either (i) a new lawsuit, or (ii) a motion for a temporary restraining order, preliminary injunction or other emergency relief in a new or existing case. The defendant must be the State of Washington, Secretary of State Steve Hobbs, or one or more Washington county election officials. The filing must seek to sequester, segregate, or bar the tabulation of ballots cast by people the department alleges are noncitizens. The filing date shown on the court docket counts. It can be confirmed by the docket (PACER/CourtListener), a Justice Department press release, a statement by the Washington Secretary of State's office, or a major outlet's report. NO if no such filing is made in the window. A filing made before 29 Oct 00:00 PT does not count, and neither do threats, letters or announced intentions without a filing.
- **Event window:** 2026-10-29T00:00:00-07:00 to 2026-10-30T23:59:59-07:00 (America/Los_Angeles)
- **Written at:** 2026-10-28T18:40Z

Why Pacific time: the filing would be made in a Washington federal court (Western or Eastern District) against Washington officials. The window ends at 23:59 local time there, which is 02:59 ET on 31 Oct.

## Facts
- **The letter.** On 26 Oct, Acting US Attorney Neil Floyd (Western District of Washington) and First Assistant US Attorney Pete Serrano (Eastern District) wrote to Hobbs. They asked the state to "take immediate action to sequester any ballots from the noncitizen voters identified" — 405 people. KOMO https://komonews.com/news/local/department-of-justice-washington-state-voters-405-non-citizens-ballots-us-attorney-pete-serrano-eastern-district-neil-floyd-doj-investigation-elections-federal-way (published 2026-10-26 19:38 PT, updated 2026-10-27 11:41, read 2026-10-28T17:56Z).
- **Hobbs's response so far.**
  - He disputes the list. He says some people on it are citizens and some records are decades old, including one from 1994. He notes that federal law bars removing people from the rolls within 90 days of an election — KOMO (as above).
  - He called the request "no doubt intended to cast unfounded suspicion on the results" — CBS (as above).
  - His office said it is "reviewing the request and assessing the information sought" — KHQ (as above).
- **California, the same pattern.**
  - The department sent California counties a similar letter with a 48-hour deadline. It then sued Secretary of State Shirley Weber and Los Angeles County officials to block tabulation of mail ballots from about 20,000 alleged noncitizens — CBS (as above).
  - Judge David Carter (C.D. Cal.) held a hearing on Tuesday 27 Oct and said he would rule on Wednesday 28 Oct.
  - On Monday 26 Oct, a 9th Circuit panel dismissed a related Justice Department appeal 2–1 for lack of jurisdiction. Judge Bennett wrote that he would also deny it on the merits.
  - Source: Stateline via KOTA https://www.kotatv.com/2026/10/28/courts-decide-fate-trumps-latest-push-control-voting-election-day-nears/ (published 2026-10-28 10:07 CDT, read 2026-10-28T17:52Z).
  - KOTA/Stateline describes Washington as the second state targeted this way. MS NOW's headline: "Washington becomes second state threatened by Justice Department over mail ballots" (search result; page not retrievable).
- **Existing cases against Washington.**
  - The department sued Hobbs on 2 Dec 2025 for the unredacted statewide voter list — MyNorthwest https://mynorthwest.com/?p=4167242 (published 2025-12-02, read 2026-10-28T17:46Z).
  - That suit was dismissed in September 2026, per the headline "Trump administration's WA voter roll lawsuit dismissed" — Washington State Standard https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2026/09/22/trump-administrations-wa-voter-roll-lawsuit-dismissed/ (search result only, not opened). Whether it was appealed was not checked.
  - Earlier this month, Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon wrote to Washington and other states warning that officials could be "criminally liable" if they knowingly let noncitizens vote. Hobbs called it "another instance in a troubling pattern of efforts to intimidate election officials" — KXLY (undated page, read 2026-10-28T17:48Z).
- **Alaska suit, context only.**
  - On 22 Oct, the department sued pre-emptively to block certification of Alaska's US Senate result if Sen. Dan Sullivan loses to Mary Peltola by fewer votes than went to a same-name candidate — ABC News https://abcnews.com/US/doj-preemptively-sues-block-alaska-senate-results-republican/story?id=137512876 (published 2026-10-23, updated 2026-10-26 17:25Z, read 2026-10-28T18:03Z).
  - The case is before Judge Sharon Gleason (D. Alaska). The Peltola campaign and the DSCC moved for fast dismissal, the state asked for a ruling by Monday 2 Nov, and Gleason set accelerated briefing "over the next few days" — KOTA/Stateline (as above).
- **Washington's ballots.** Washington votes entirely by mail. Ballots are counted from election night onward and are already arriving.

## Base rate

## Schedule slip
- Slip class: none. This is not a scheduled event, but there is a stated deadline (noon 28 Oct) and an explicit threat to "seek emergency relief". The main timing risks:
  - The department may file on 28 Oct itself, before the window. That makes this claim NO unless a further emergency motion follows in the window.
  - It may wait for Carter's California ruling or the 9th Circuit before filing.
  - It may file after the window, for example on the weekend or once tabulation begins on 3 Nov.

## Risks
- **Filing on 28 Oct (before the window):** the most likely way an "obvious YES" story resolves NO.
- **California outcome:** if Carter rules against the department on 28 Oct, it may hold off in Washington, which is also in the 9th Circuit, or may file anyway to build an appeal record.
- **Partial compliance:** Hobbs or counties may agree to flag or review the 405 records (for example, by challenging ballots under state procedure), removing the need to sue.
- **Second list:** a "second list" is reportedly coming (Lynnwood Times headline). A suit might wait for it.
- **Venue:** the filing could be made in either Washington district, or added to another case. The criterion covers any federal court.
- **Counter-filing:** a filing by Washington against the department, for example a declaratory action, does not count.

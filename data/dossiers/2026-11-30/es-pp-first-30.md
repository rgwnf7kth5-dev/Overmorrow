# Dossier es-pp-first-30

Claim (secondary; depends on es-majority-30): The People's Party wins the most seats in the Congress of Deputies in Spain's general election on Sunday 29 November 2026.

Criterion (draft): YES if, in the Interior Ministry's provisional results (resultados.29neleccionesgenerales.es) with 100% of polling-station returns processed (or the highest share processed, at least 99%, when read at 05:00 CET on 30 Nov), the PP's candidacies together hold more Congress seats than any other party's candidacies together. PP candidacies are all Partido Popular lists in every constituency (including Partit Popular / Partido Popular lists) and the joint list "Unión del Pueblo Navarro en coalición con el Partido Popular" (UPN–PP) in Navarre; PSOE candidacies likewise include PSC, PSdeG, PSE-EE, PSN and other PSOE federation lists. A tie for most seats counts as NO. NO also if another party has more seats, or if the election is not held on 29 Nov 2026 (Europe/Madrid). The definitive count (4–7 Dec, adding votes from abroad) and later challenges do not change the resolution. If the ministry's site cannot be read, the ministry's provisional seat totals as reported by RTVE or EFE decide.

## Risks

- Narrowing lead: three of 16 private pollsters have PP ahead by about one point or less (40dB +0.9, Ipsos +0.5, Ateneo +1.3) and one has PSOE ahead (SW Demoscopia); the last four days are unpolled publicly.
- 2023-style overstatement of PP (up to 5 points in some polls, per Electomanía) would erase a 2.9-point lead in votes; seats need a larger swing because PP's seat efficiency is higher (2023: 1.4 points → 16 seats).
- PSOE concentration of the left vote (Frente Amplio 4–7%, Podemos ~1%) and Catalonia (PSC) could add seats beyond national-share models.
- Wording: UPN–PP seats (likely 1–2) count for PP; a tie for first counts as NO; the ministry site may show "UPN-PP" as a separate line.

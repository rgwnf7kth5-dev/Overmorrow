# il-speaker-request-11
- **Claim:** By 23:59 Israel time on Tuesday 10 November 2026 at least 61 members of the 26th Knesset submit a signed request to the Knesset presidium or speaker for an early vote to elect a new speaker.
- **Criterion:** YES if the Knesset, the Jerusalem Post, Times of Israel, Haaretz, JNS or Ynet report that between 00:00 on 9 Nov and 23:59 on 10 Nov 2026 (Israel time) a request signed by at least 61 Knesset members, asking for a vote to elect the Knesset speaker, was submitted (handed to the speaker, the presidium or the Knesset secretariat). A request with fewer than 61 signatures, signatures still being collected, or a submission on 11 Nov or later is NO. Also YES if on 10 Nov the Knesset actually elects a speaker by a vote in which at least 61 members voted for the winner other than Amir Ohana.
- **Deadline:** window 2026-11-09T00:00:00+02:00 to 2026-11-10T23:59:00+02:00
- **Written at (UTC):** 2026-11-08T18:05Z by the desk editor. The facts below are those gathered for il-speaker-11 (same story); they apply unchanged to this claim. The original claim of that dossier is kept as the story's second claim.

## Original header of il-speaker-11
- **Claim:** At a sitting on 10 November 2026 (the opening session of the 26th Knesset or any later sitting that day) the Knesset elects a Knesset speaker, and the person elected is not Amir Ohana.
- **Criterion:** YES if, by 23:59 Israel time on 10 Nov 2026, the Knesset plenum votes to elect a speaker and the elected speaker is anyone other than Amir Ohana, as shown on the Knesset website or reported by the Jerusalem Post, Times of Israel, Haaretz, JNS or Ynet. NO if no speaker is elected on 10 Nov 2026 (including a vote only scheduled or signatures only collected that day), or if Ohana is elected or confirmed.

## Schedule
- The 26th Knesset is sworn in on Tuesday 10 Nov: "The inaugural ceremony for the 26th Knesset is set to take place on Tuesday, regardless of whether a coalition government has been formed" — https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-913918 (published 2026-11-05T11:43Z, read 2026-11-08T17:50Z).
- No speaker election is on the announced agenda. Ohana's position: the date of the speaker election "would be set only when the new government is formed, unless a majority of Knesset members sign a letter requesting that it be scheduled earlier" — JPost 913918 above. Later: "If a majority of them request such a discussion, I will schedule it for an earlier date, no later than one week" — https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-914176 (published 2026-11-08T10:02Z, read 2026-11-08T17:51Z). Haaretz (5 Nov): Ohana "would convene a vote on his replacement within a week of receiving signatures from at least 61 lawmakers" — https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/elections/2026-11-05/ty-article/.premium/netanyahu-knesset-speaker-rejects-bid-to-replace-him-during-knesset-swearing-in/000001a1-ab35-d5c5-ade1-bf7ff7a90000 (published 2026-11-05T12:47+02:00, read 2026-11-08T17:51Z).
- The opposition plans to start collecting signatures that day: Liberman: "Next Tuesday, we will act to collect signatures to replace the Knesset speaker" — https://www.jfeed.com/news/change-bloc-replace-speaker-eisenkot-mandate (published 2026-11-03T11:24Z, read 2026-11-08T17:51Z). The four opposition parties (Yashar, Yesh Atid, Yisrael Beytenu, Democrats) asked Ohana to hold the debate and vote "as early as Tuesday" — JPost 913918.

## Facts
- Herzog tasked Eisenkot with forming a government on Sun 8 Nov at the President's Residence — https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-914236 (published 2026-11-08T16:59Z, read 2026-11-08T17:51Z) "officially received the mandate ... during a ceremony at the President's Residence".
- Majority exists on paper: 67 MKs recommended Eisenkot, 46 Netanyahu — JPost 914176 above "67 MKs who do not support Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu compared with 46 who do". The 67 includes the Arab parties (Ra'am, Joint List): "their recommendations do not commit them to ultimately joining his coalition" — JPost 914236. The four-party opposition bloc alone has 48 seats (Jfeed 3 Nov above); 61 signatures therefore need Arab-party MKs; Liberman "would not support forming a government that relies on the Joint List or Ra'am abstaining" (https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-914045, published 2026-11-06T10:50Z, read 2026-11-07T17:53Z).
- No agreed candidate (as of 5 Nov): "the opposition bloc has not agreed on a candidate to replace Ohana. Party leaders ... set next Monday [9 Nov] as the deadline for a decision." Liberman backs Lapid; Eisenkot "wants to become Knesset speaker himself" — JPost 913918. Jfeed 5 Nov: Bennett and Liberman back Lapid, Eisenkot wants a Yashar candidate — https://www.jfeed.com/news/opposition-vote-replace-knesset-speaker-ohana (published 2026-11-05T09:19Z, read 2026-11-08T17:52Z). Yashar sources on 3 Nov: "Lapid will not be Knesset speaker" (Jfeed 3 Nov above). Oded Forer (Yisrael Beytenu) was also floated (Jfeed 3 Nov). One proposal: Eisenkot would resign as speaker if he becomes prime minister (JPost 913918).
- Likud counter-moves alleged: opposition sources say hundreds of Likud Central Committee members plan to come to the Knesset on the day of the vote to block MKs and disrupt the session; Likud had not confirmed — JPost 913918.
- Ynet 8 Nov headline: "Eisenkot delays government swearing-in until Knesset speaker is replaced" (he wants Ohana out before bringing a government to a confidence vote) — https://www.ynetnews.com/article/bjvjdbatfe (published 2026-11-08T14:38Z, read 2026-11-08T17:51Z; only the deck was rendered). Ynet 8 Nov: "the new Knesset will be sworn in Tuesday, with lawmakers preparing to replace its speaker" — https://www.ynetnews.com/article/r1aw0l6tmx (published 2026-11-08T02:46Z, read 2026-11-08T17:52Z).
- Rules: per the JPost explainer, "Section 2(b) of the Knesset Rules of Procedure ... stipulates that the Knesset speaker must be elected no later than the date on which the Knesset convenes for the purpose of forming a government"; an outgoing speaker serves "by virtue of the principle of continuity"; and the plenum agenda is set by the speaker — JPost 914176. The Knesset's own website page on the speaker could not be read (https://main.knesset.gov.il/en/about/lawsandplenum/pages/speaker.aspx returned an empty page). So a permanent speaker is not automatically elected on the day of swearing-in; the election needs a plenum agenda item.
- Precedent, 2020: Knesset sworn in 16 Mar 2020; 61 MKs asked for a speaker vote at the swearing-in; Edelstein refused; the High Court ordered a vote; Edelstein resigned; Gantz was later chosen — JPost 914176. So the previous attempt did not produce a speaker election on swearing-in day.

## Risks
- Ohana may convene a vote within the week after signatures (Wed 11 Nov or later) which is NO.
- Ohana could agree to a vote the same day if the signatures are delivered before the session; the opposition has not confirmed it has 61 signatures or a candidate.
- A vote can be held up by disruption, procedural motions or a court petition.
- If a vote is held and the candidate has only 48 + Arab-party votes, a secret ballot could produce surprises; Ohana could also be re-elected only if some opposition MKs defect (unlikely).
- Time zone: the vote must happen by 23:59 Israel time on 10 Nov.

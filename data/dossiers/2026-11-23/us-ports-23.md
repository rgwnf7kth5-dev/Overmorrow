# us-ports-23
- **Claim:** The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26 announce a tentative agreement or a further contract extension for the port watchmen at Los Angeles and Long Beach by 23:59 Pacific time on Sunday 22 November 2026.
- **Criterion:** YES if, between 20:00 PT on Fri 20 Nov 2026 (05:00 CET Sat 21 Nov, the freeze) and 23:59 PT on Sun 22 Nov 2026, the Pacific Maritime Association (PMA) or the ILWU (Local 26 or the International) announces, or Reuters, the Los Angeles Times, the Daily Breeze, gCaptain, the Journal of Commerce or FreightWaves report on the authority of either party or the federal mediators (FMCS), that PMA and ILWU Local 26 have reached a tentative agreement on a new watchmen contract or have agreed to extend the existing contract for any period. An extension or tentative agreement reached after a strike has begun also counts. A ratification vote is not needed. NO if neither is announced in that window, including when a strike continues or when the contract lapses without a strike and talks continue with no extension. Missing reports leave the claim pending, not NO.
- **Event window:** 2026-11-20T20:00:00-08:00 to 2026-11-22T23:59:00-08:00 (America/Los_Angeles)
- **Evidence expected:** A PMA statement (pmanet.org press releases, PMA social media) and agency copy (Reuters) within hours of any deal. Earlier extensions were announced the same night.
- **Deadline (resolves by):** 2026-11-24T05:00:00+01:00
- **Written at:** 2026-11-20T18:20:00Z (10:20 PT Fri 20 Nov, while the last scheduled session was due that day)

## Facts

Also: "The union has indicated an unwillingness to extend the contract beyond its expiration at 12:01 a.m. on Saturday, November 21, setting up the real potential for a strike at the nation's busiest port complex."
- Reuters (Lisa Baertlein) via Baird Maritime, 18 Nov — https://www.bairdmaritime.com/shipping/ports/union-talks-stall-at-los-angeles-and-long-beach-ports-strike-looms (published 2026-11-18T18:16Z, read 2026-11-20T18:06:01Z) "The two sides had previously avoided walkouts through three consecutive short-term extensions … The ILWU watchmen, who already have voted to authorise a strike, did not immediately comment."
- Scope, from gCaptain citing PMA, 18 Nov — https://gcaptain.com/strike-threat-looms-at-ports-of-los-angeles-and-long-beach-as-labor-talks-stall/ (published 2026-11-18 09:08 PST, read 2026-11-20T18:06:05Z). About 250 watchmen guard "six container terminals responsible for more than half of the cargo … approximately 85% of Long Beach's cargo and 33% of Los Angeles' cargo". Also: "Thousands of longshore workers, marine clerks and foremen covered by separate labor agreements are expected to honor Local 26 picket lines".
- PMA's offer (same gCaptain article): "a one-time $3,200 bonus … wage increases that would raise average annual earnings for full-time watchmen from more than $170,000 to $198,000"; employer-paid healthcare "valued at more than $62,000 annually"; pensions up to $93,200. The previous agreement expired in July 2025, and the dispute is separate from the coastwide longshore contract, which runs through 2028.
- PMA on 27 Oct, quoted by DTN (as above): "As of Oct. 27, every tentative agreement reached has been on a Union proposal. None of the Employers' proposals have been resolved, and significant differences remain."
- The first extension came on 1 Nov. The contract had been due to expire on 31 Oct, with a possible strike on Sun 1 Nov, and was extended to 5 p.m. Wed 4 Nov — https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/talks-continue-watchmen-ports-la-183951366.html (published 2026-11-01T18:39Z, read 2026-11-20T18:06:30Z) "The Pacific Maritime Association said in a statement on social media that it had signed an agreement with International Longshore and Warehouse Union Local 26 to extend their existing contract through 5 p.m. on Wednesday, Nov. 4."
- The second extension, on 4 Nov, ran to midnight Fri 6 Nov — https://www.freightwaves.com/news/port-strike-delayed-again-at-los-angeles-long-beach-ports (published 2026-11-05T11:00Z, read 2026-11-20T18:06:32Z) "The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26 have agreed to extend their contract through midnight Pacific Time on Friday night, November 6".
- The third extension, on 6–7 Nov, ran to 20 Nov — https://www.bairdmaritime.com/shipping/ports/another-extension-delays-potential-strike-at-los-angeles-long-beach-ports (Reuters, published 2026-11-09T09:26Z, read 2026-11-20T18:06:15Z) "extended their bargaining deadline through November 20". ### Facts bearing on the alternative claim (a strike halts work at at least one terminal on Sun 22 Nov)
- For it: the strike has been authorised; per PMA the union is unwilling to extend; PMA reports "little-to-no movement"; and PMA, Crane and DTN all say other ILWU workers are expected not to cross Local 26 picket lines (above). The 2012 clerks' strike shows a small unit's picket can stop terminals.
- Against it, or making it harder to resolve: three of three earlier deadlines ended in extensions. A strike also needs the union to walk out at or after 00:01 Sat, and longshore workers to honour the line on a Sunday shift. No union strike notice or picket plan was published. An arbitrator's ruling on the picket line is possible under the longshore contract (not checked in this dossier).

## Risks
- **For NO:** the union keeps to its stated refusal to extend; a strike starts on Sat or Sun and no deal comes before Sunday night; or the contract lapses and talks continue with no formal extension (that is NO under this criterion).
- **For YES:** the pattern of last-minute extensions, FMCS mediation, political pressure from Rep. Barragán and peak holiday-season volumes. A strike could start and then end with a deal or a return-to-work extension before Sunday night (YES).
- **Timing:** if PMA announces a deal or extension on Friday before 20:00 PT (05:00 CET, the freeze), the outcome is known before the window opens. The morning desk should then drop or replace the claim; do not resolve it on a pre-window announcement.
- **Ambiguity:** a "handshake" or "framework" without the words "tentative agreement" or "extension". Treat it as YES only if either party or the FMCS says the strike is averted or called off because terms have been agreed or the contract continues. An agreement only to keep talking, while watchmen stay out or the contract has lapsed, is NO.

# eu-dfa-fines-12
- **Claim:** The Commission's consumer package of 11 November 2026 proposes that the Commission itself can fine traders for breaches of EU consumer law, with a maximum of at least 6% of worldwide annual turnover.
- **Criterion:** YES if a legislative proposal adopted and made public by the Commission on 11 Nov 2026 (the Digital Fairness Act or the consumer enforcement initiative) gives the Commission power to impose fines directly on traders for consumer-law infringements, with a maximum of at least 6% of the trader's worldwide (global) annual turnover, as shown by the proposal text, the Commission press release or Q&A, or reports by major outlets. NO if the package is adopted that day but contains no direct Commission fining power, or the maximum is below 6% of worldwide turnover, or if no such proposal is adopted on 11 Nov.

## Risks
- Centralised Commission enforcement is the most contested power shift (member-state authorities run consumer enforcement today); it may be cut or narrowed at College level or the cap lowered.
- The power may be limited to "widespread infringements with a Union dimension"; that still counts if the Commission can fine directly.
- Fines may be set as a percentage of EU turnover rather than worldwide turnover — that would be NO; resolvers must check the text.
- The enforcement initiative could be split from the DFA and adopted later.

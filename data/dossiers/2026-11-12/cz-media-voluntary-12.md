# cz-media-voluntary-12
- **Claim:** Between 06:00 on 10 November and 23:59 on 11 November 2026, Prime Minister Babiš, Finance Minister Schillerová, Culture Minister Klempíř, SPD leader Okamura or Motoristé leader Macinka publicly states that the coalition's new model for funding Czech Television and Czech Radio will include voluntary payments by citizens.
- **Criterion:** YES if, between 2026-11-10T06:00 and 2026-11-11T23:59 Prague time, at least one of Andrej Babiš, Alena Schillerová, Oto Klempíř, Tomio Okamura or Petr Macinka publicly states (press conference, interview, social-media post or press release) that the funding model agreed or proposed by the coalition for ČT and ČRo after the abolition of the licence fees will include voluntary payments or contributions by citizens (e.g. a voluntary fee or voluntary contributions to an account), as reported by ČTK (ceskenoviny.cz), ČT24, Seznam Zprávy, Novinky, iDNES, Aktuálně, Deník N or the speaker's own channel. A statement that such payments are merely being considered or discussed, or that they have been dropped, does not count. NO if no such statement is made in the window.
- **Deadline:** 2026-11-11T23:59+01:00; evidence by 2026-11-12T05:00+01:00
- **Written at (UTC):** 2026-11-09T18:25Z. Read times as stated.
- **Status at writing:** the outcome of the coalition council of Monday evening 9 Nov had not been reported by 18:20Z (front pages of ceskenoviny.cz, ct24.ceskatelevize.cz, novinky.cz, idnes.cz, denikn.cz checked). The claim must be re-checked against that outcome before the freeze (see Risks).

## Risks
- The coalition council may drop voluntary payments (e.g. choose a fixed budget amount or VAT share only) or postpone the decision; then statements would say it is "considered" or rejected: NO.
- Leaders may announce the outcome only on the evening of 9 Nov and not repeat it in the window; repetition in the window is needed.
- Wording such as "zvažujeme" or "jedna z možností" does not count; borderline wording may need the full quote.
- Other news (Macinka controversy, sector taxes) may crowd out media-funding statements.

# ariane-24
- **Claim:** Ariane 6 (Ariane 64) lifts off from Kourou on flight VA271 with 36 Amazon Leo satellites on Monday 23 November 2026 and Arianespace declares the mission successful, with the satellites separated as planned.
- **Criterion:** YES if Ariane 6 flight VA271 (Amazon Leo LE-04) lifts off from the Guiana Space Centre in Kourou between 00:00 and 23:59 Kourou time (UTC−3) on 23 Nov 2026 AND, by 23:59 Kourou time on 23 Nov, Arianespace (or ESA/CNES) announces that the mission was successful / that the Amazon Leo satellites were separated (deployed) as planned. Evidence: arianespace.com / newsroom.arianespace.com, ESA, CNES, or an established outlet (SpaceNews, Spaceflight Now, NASASpaceflight, AFP/Reuters, Le Monde, Space.com) reporting the Arianespace statement. NO if the launch slips beyond 23:59 Kourou time on 23 Nov (scrub, hold or postponement), or if the vehicle fails or a partial failure leaves the satellites not separated as planned. Later problems with individual satellites (contact, orbit raising) do not count against YES.
- **Why success, not just lift-off:** the mission lasts "approximately two hours and four minutes, from liftoff to the deployment of the final satellites" (Arianespace press release, below). With a window of 08:09–08:38 Kourou, deployment ends by about 10:45 Kourou at the latest, and Arianespace has announced each previous Amazon Leo mission's success the same day (e.g. VA269 press release dated 17 June 2026, the launch day — cited in Wikipedia, below). So the success announcement falls well inside the window, and success is the fuller open question.
- **Event window:** 2026-11-23T00:00:00-03:00 to 2026-11-23T23:59:00-03:00 (America/Cayenne). Launch window 08:09–08:38 Kourou = 11:09–11:38 UTC = 12:09–12:38 CET.
- **Evidence expected:** Arianespace live stream and press release "Arianespace successfully launches …" on 23 Nov, around 10:15–11:00 Kourou (13:15–14:00 UTC); ESA/CNES releases. evidence_at 2026-11-23T11:30:00-03:00.

## Risks
- **Same-day scrub or hold** past the 29-minute window (weather, ground systems, launcher anomaly) → slips at least a day → NO.
- **Launch readiness review** (expected 22 Nov) could postpone.
- **Vehicle/upper-stage problem:** VA262's upper-stage APU anomaly shows the Vinci stage's multi-burn deployment is the riskier phase; 36 satellites deployed over about two hours.
- **Turbopump issue recurrence:** the September anomaly affected two or three engines; the VA271 engine was re-tested nominal.
- **Announcement timing:** success is normally announced within an hour or so of deployment; a delay beyond 23:59 Kourou would make the claim NO only if no confirmation exists by then — very unlikely.

# ofgem-26
- **Claim:** On 25 November 2026 Ofgem announces a default tariff (energy price) cap for 1 January – 31 March 2027 of at least £2,154 a year for a typical dual-fuel household paying by Direct Debit, i.e. a rise of at least 25% on the current £1,723.
- **Criterion:** YES if Ofgem, on 25 Nov 2026 (UK time), announces the 1 Jan – 31 Mar 2027 cap and the annual cost it states for a typical household using electricity and gas and paying by Direct Debit (Ofgem's typical domestic consumption values) is £2,154 or more. The figure is read from Ofgem's announcement or its "Summary of changes" document; if Ofgem's page gives only a percentage, the £ figure reported the same day from Ofgem's documents by Cornwall Insight, the BBC or the Guardian decides. NO if the figure is £2,153 or less, or if Ofgem announces no cap for that period on 25 Nov 2026. (If Ofgem publishes before 25 Nov the claim cannot be met in the window: see Schedule.)
- **Event window:** 2026-11-25T00:00:00+00:00 to 2026-11-25T23:59:00+00:00 (Europe/London); usual announcement time 07:00 GMT (08:00 CET), not confirmed by Ofgem.
- **Evidence expected:** Ofgem news page "Changes to energy price cap between 1 January and 31 March 2027" and Cornwall Insight's same-day press release.
- **Deadline (resolves by):** 2026-11-27T05:00:00+01:00
- **Written at:** 2026-11-23T18:10:00Z

## Facts
- Current cap (1 Oct – 31 Dec 2026): £1,723 a year for a typical dual-fuel Direct Debit household, +4% — Cornwall Insight https://www.cornwall-insight.com/press-releases/bills-to-climb-in-october-with-worse-to-come-in-january/ (published 2026-08-26T06:23Z, read 2026-11-23T17:51:40Z) "£1,723 October price cap announced today"; Ofgem page above "energy prices will go up by 4% for a typical household". VAT removed from electricity 1 Oct 2026 – 31 Mar 2027 (Ofgem page).
- Cornwall Insight final forecast for Jan–Mar 2027: £2,166 a year, +£443, +26%, highest since 2023; VAT removal on electricity (extended to July 2027) priced in; £100 Household Energy Payment not included; possible removal of gas standing charges in 2027 not included — https://www.cornwall-insight.com/press-releases/energy-bills-set-for-steep-rise-in-january/ (published 2026-11-18T07:59Z, read 2026-11-23T17:51:28Z) "The annual cap for a typical consumer is expected to reach £2,166, an increase of £443 on the current cap."
- Drivers: Middle East conflict, disrupted gas exports, European gas storage at record lows (same Cornwall release) "left European gas storage at record lows heading into winter".
- Forecast path for this cap: 26 Aug £1,872 (+9%); 30 Sep £1,999 (+16%); 18 Nov final £2,166 (+26%) — Cornwall releases above and https://www.cornwall-insight.com/press-releases/16-price-cap-rise-forecast-in-january/ (scout note, 30 Sep; title read on the press-release index 2026-11-23T17:51Z).
- Uswitch: "analysts expect ~27% rise" (scout note; https://www.uswitch.com/gas-electricity/guides/price-cap/, modified 16 Nov 2026; not re-read tonight).
- Threshold arithmetic: 1.25 × £1,723 = £2,153.75 → £2,154. Cornwall's £2,166 is £12 above it (+25.7%).

## Schedule slip
- Class used: `stats-release` (a regulated, formula-driven figure published on a stated date): rate 0.002 (prior 0.01, own 0/69) — `node scripts/refdata/slip.mjs stats-release`, run 2026-11-23T17:51Z. Alternative `other-scheduled` 0.094 (11/116) is a poor fit for a statutory quarterly publication.
- Specific risk: early publication. Ofgem says "by 25 November" and "may publish before this date"; the last three announcements came on the stated day (the stated date for the 21 Nov 2025 announcement was not checked). If the cap is published on 24 Nov the claim falls outside the window and must be re-planned in the morning run.

## Risks
- Methodology or policy changes in the cap not in Cornwall's model (network charges, levies, Budget measures announced in November 2026). The £100 Household Energy Payment sits outside the cap and does not affect the figure.
- Ofgem changing the TDCVs or the headline presentation (e.g. stating only %); criterion handles this via Ofgem's documents.
- Wholesale moves after Cornwall's model are irrelevant: the observation window has closed (Cornwall final forecast follows its close).

# cv-neves-16
- **Claim:** José Maria Neves wins more than 50% of the valid votes in the first round of the Cabo Verde presidential election on Sunday 15 November 2026.
- **Criterion:** YES if the provisional results of the 15 Nov 2026 first round published by Cabo Verde's electoral authorities (CNE results page presidenciais2026.cne.cv/resultados, or DGAPE provisional counts as reported by Inforpress, RTC, Expresso das Ilhas or Lusa), with all or nearly all (at least 95%) polling stations in the country and the diaspora counted, show José Maria Neves with more than 50% of the valid votes. If the CNE's general count (apuramento geral) is published before the resolver reads, it takes precedence. NO if he has 50% or less (a runoff on 29 Nov), or if the first round is not held on 15 Nov 2026.
- **Event window:** polling 2026-11-15T08:00−01:00 to 18:00−01:00 (Atlantic/Cape_Verde, UTC−1); window closes 2026-11-15T23:59−01:00; provisional national figures expected the same night; evidence by 2026-11-16T06:00−01:00; resolver reads 2026-11-17T05:00+01:00.

## Facts
- Six candidates: José Maria Neves (incumbent, backed by PAICV), Joana Rosa (ex-justice minister, backed by MpD, first woman to run), and four without major-party backing: Gilson Alves (doctor), Casimiro Pina (jurist), Antero Tavares (judge), Loisito de Sousa (teacher) — Expresso das Ilhas/Lusa (as above) "seis candidatos na corrida, dois dos quais apoiados pelos principais partidos do país".
- Casimiro de Pina finished third in 2021 — Rio Times https://www.riotimesonline.com/cabo-verde-presidential-vote-two-weeks-out-what-to-watch/ (published 2026-11-01, modified 2026-11-10, read 2026-11-13T17:54Z) "Casimiro de Pina finished third in 2021."
- Electorate: 419,448 registered (343,065 at home, 76,383 abroad = 18.2%); 1,302 polling stations (1,059 at home, 243 abroad); Praia 91,496 voters — Inforpress https://inforpress.cv/article-12414 (published 2026-11-13, read 2026-11-13T17:53Z) "A total of 419,448 voters are registered".
- About 150 observers from six international organisations — Expresso das Ilhas/Lusa (as above).
- 2021 first round (17 Oct 2021): Neves (PAICV) 96,035 votes = 51.79% of valid votes, Carlos Veiga (MpD) 78,603 = 42.39%, others ≈5.8%; registered 398,690, turnout 47.99% — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Cape_Verdean_presidential_election (modified 2026-09-12, read 2026-11-13T17:53Z). In 2021 Neves ran in cohabitation with an MpD government.
- 2021 timing: "Late on election day, Veiga conceded defeat" — same Wikipedia page; i.e. provisional counts were clear the same night.
- 17 May 2026 legislative election: PAICV 90,660 votes = 48.04% (37 of 72 seats, majority), MpD 84,458 = 44.75% (33), UCID 9,812 = 5.20% (2); PAICV's Francisco Carvalho became prime minister — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cape_Verdean_parliamentary_election (read 2026-11-13T17:53Z) "PAICV … won a majority of seats, unseating the Movement for Democracy (MpD)". So Neves now runs alongside a PAICV government (no cohabitation).
- December 2024 local elections: PAICV won 14 municipalities including Praia, first time on top in local elections since 2000 — same page.
- Polls: no published opinion poll from a named institute found — Rio Times (two-weeks-out page, as above) "No independent opinion poll from a named institute could be found." Own search 13 Nov (WebSearch "sondagem presidenciais 2026 Cabo Verde …") found none.
- Abstention in 2021 was 52% — Expresso das Ilhas/Lusa (as above).

## Base rate

Rate 0.71, n=7. (1991 from general knowledge, not re-read.)
- Sub-class incumbent seeking re-election: 3 of 3 won in the first round (1996, 2006, 2016); both runoffs (2001, 2011) were open-seat contests between the two big parties' nominees. n is tiny.
- Neves's 2021 margin over 50% was 1.8 points; PAICV's May 2026 legislative share was 48.0% (with UCID 5.2% and others outside the two big parties).

## Risks
- No polls: the outcome rests on the 2021 result, the May 2026 legislative vote and incumbency.
- Six candidates instead of two main ones in 2021 plus minor ones: four minor candidates together took ≈5.8% in 2021; if they take more, Neves needs a larger lead over Rosa to clear 50%.
- Diaspora (18% of the roll) counts arrive later and differ by region; early provisional numbers from Santiago may overstate or understate the final share.
- Provisional vs official: a share very close to 50% (as in 2001: 50.01% in round 2) could flip between provisional and general count; criterion uses the general count if published before resolution.
- Turnout: abstention 52% in 2021; mobilisation by the new PAICV government vs MpD after its May defeat.

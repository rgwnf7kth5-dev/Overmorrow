# tw-nuclear-29 — Taiwan Referendum No. 22 (nuclear power)

Claim: Taiwan's national Referendum No. 22 on nuclear power, held with the local elections on Saturday 28 November 2026, passes.

Criterion: YES if the Central Election Commission's count for Referendum No. 22 held on 28 Nov 2026 shows more valid 'agree' votes than 'disagree' votes AND 'agree' votes of at least one quarter of all eligible voters (20,003,835 eligible per the CEC on 24 Nov 2026, so at least 5,000,959 agree votes), i.e. the referendum passes (通過) under Article 29 of the Referendum Act. NO otherwise, or if the referendum is not held on 28 Nov 2026 (Asia/Taipei). If the CEC site cannot be read, Focus Taiwan/CNA reporting of the CEC result decides.

Deadline: event window 2026-11-28T08:00:00+08:00 to 2026-11-28T23:59:00+08:00 (polls open 08:00-16:00); evidence_at 2026-11-28T23:00:00+08:00 (see Risks: counting may run to about midnight); resolves_by 2026-11-29T05:00:00+01:00.

Written: 2026-11-26T18:05:00Z

- 2018-11-24 (Nos. 7-16, held with local elections): 7 adopted (7-12, 16), 3 rejected (13-15). No. 16 (repeal the 2025 nuclear-phaseout deadline): agree 5,895,560 (59.49%), disagree 4,014,215; electorate 19,757,067; turnout 54.83%; ADOPTED — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Taiwanese_referendum (read 2026-11-26T17:57Z)

Arithmetic (not a forecast): with 20,003,835 eligible, the threshold is 25.0% of the electorate. In 2018 No. 16 agree votes equalled 29.8% of the electorate at 54.8% turnout; in 2025 alone, 21.7% at 29.5% turnout. At a referendum turnout of 55%, the agree share of valid votes would need to be roughly 46% or more (somewhat higher allowing for invalid ballots) to reach the threshold.

## Base rate

All ten are from one day (24 Nov 2018), so the cases are not independent. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Taiwanese_referendum
- Wider class: all Taiwanese national referendum questions since the 2017/2018 reform under the 25% rule (2018: 7/10; 2021: 0/4; 2025: 0/1): 7 passed of 15 (rate 0.47, n = 15). Sources: 2018, 2021 and 2025 Wikipedia referendum pages above. Difference: this question is held with local elections (as in 2018, not 2021/2025) and on nuclear power, where 'agree' led in 2018 and 2025 but lost narrowly in 2021.

## Risks

- Turnout: the referendum ballot is handed out separately and can be declined; if fewer local-election voters take it, or turnout falls below 2018 levels (2018: 54.8%), agree votes may stay below 5,000,959 even with a large agree majority.
- A 'disagree' surge: in 2021 the DPP mobilised 'no' and nuclear restart lost 47-53. If DPP voters vote 'disagree' rather than abstain, agree votes may fail to exceed disagree.
- Lai's softer nuclear stance and the ongoing Maanshan restart review may reduce urgency for 'agree' voters, or reduce DPP voters' 'disagree' turnout; the direction is unclear.
- Resolution timing: counting of referendum ballots is expected to end around midnight; at evidence_at 23:00 the count may be incomplete.
- Source confusion: one outlet (TVBS) printed a 4,887,133 threshold; the criterion should use 5,000,959 from the CEC electorate count. If the final agree total falls between the two numbers, reports may disagree; the CEC 通過/不通過 status decides.

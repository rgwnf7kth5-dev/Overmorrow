# Dossier itlos-sealion-29

Claim: In its order delivered on Saturday 28 November 2026, the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea prescribes at least one provisional measure in Argentina's case against the United Kingdom over the Sea Lion oil project.

Criterion (draft): YES if the operative part of the ITLOS order delivered on 28 Nov 2026 on Argentina's request for provisional measures prescribes at least one provisional measure under article 290 of UNCLOS, whether one Argentina requested or a different one the Tribunal sets of its own motion (for example an obligation on the parties to consult, exchange information or report, or to refrain from action). NO if the Tribunal rejects the request, finds that it lacks prima facie jurisdiction (including under article 298 declarations), prescribes nothing, or does not deliver the order on 28 Nov 2026 (Europe/Berlin). Recitals urging the parties without an operative prescription do not count.

Proposed criterion tweak (see claims JSON): count only an operative paragraph that "prescribes" a provisional measure; a stand-alone "decides" paragraph on reports or costs, or a "finds that the circumstances ... are not such as to require" formula, is NO.

Deadline: event window 2026-11-28T00:00:00+01:00 to 2026-11-28T23:59:00+01:00 (order read at 12:00 Hamburg); evidence_at 2026-11-28T13:00:00+01:00; resolves_by 2026-11-29T05:00:00+01:00. Case: ar-uk-sealion-itlos (new).

- How this case differs: the respondent has given formal written assurances that nothing (drilling) happens before 21 Mar 2027, and the Annex VII tribunal may be constituted within weeks — the exact "no urgency before constitution" pattern; against that, in MOX Plant (UK assurances on shipments) and Land Reclamation (Singapore commitments) the Tribunal still prescribed a consultation/cooperation measure of its own motion. Jurisdiction here also turns on the novel land-sovereignty ("real issue") objection, which none of the 14 earlier cases raised in this form.

## Risks

- NO path 1 (most likely failure mode): no urgency — Tribunal takes note of the UK's 5 Nov assurances (no drilling before 21 Mar 2027, no extraction before Mar 2028, no new licences) and the imminent constitution of the Annex VII tribunal, and "finds that the circumstances ... are not such as to require" measures, as in Zheng He (2024).
- NO path 2: no prima facie jurisdiction — Tribunal accepts that the real issue is sovereignty over the Falklands (Chagos reasoning). ITLOS has never denied prima facie jurisdiction in a PM order, but no earlier case put a land-sovereignty objection this squarely.
- YES path: as in MOX/Land Reclamation, the Tribunal prescribes something milder of its own motion — e.g. cooperation/consultation, information exchange, or non-aggravation — or records the UK assurances as a prescribed measure. Wording matters: a paragraph that merely "places on record" or "takes note of" assurances is NO under the criterion; a "Decides that the parties shall submit a report" paragraph exists only alongside prescribed measures.
- Delivery: date could in principle slip; no indication.
- Source conflict on drilling start: MercoPress "January 2027" vs UK assurance "not before 21 March 2027"; the latter is the formal statement to the Tribunal.

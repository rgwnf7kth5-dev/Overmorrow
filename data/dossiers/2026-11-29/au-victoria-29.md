# Dossier au-victoria-29

Claim: The Liberal–National Coalition led by Jess Wilson wins a majority of at least 45 of the 88 seats in the Victorian Legislative Assembly at the state election on Saturday 28 November 2026.

Criterion (draft): YES if the Liberal and National parties together have won, or lead the count in, at least 45 of the 88 Legislative Assembly seats according to the Victorian Electoral Commission's results (results.vec.vic.gov.au), or, where the VEC count is incomplete, the ABC's Victorian election results page, as read at 12:00 CET on Tuesday 1 December 2026. NO if they hold or lead in 44 seats or fewer, or if the election is not held on 28 Nov 2026 (Australia/Melbourne).

Proposed tightening (see claims JSON): count Liberal and National candidates (including any three-cornered contests) only; a seat whose election fails or is deferred counts as not won; "lead the count" = ahead on the VEC/ABC two-candidate count, or ABC projected winner where no two-candidate count is published.

## Risks

- One Nation wins seats the pendulum assigns to the Coalition (outer-suburban and Nationals-held regional seats); MRP and models put ONP at 11–12 seats, so the Coalition's path runs through Labor seats further up the pendulum than Monbulk.
- Teal / independent and Greens contests in Coalition-held or target seats (Kew, Hawthorn, Mornington, Mildura, Prahran, Narracan; inner-city seats not on Coalition path).
- Polling error: no Australian state benchmark in our data; ONP's vote shifted 5 points between DemosAU waves with a change in question format (candidate list), showing method sensitivity.
- Count timing: on 1 Dec (22:00 AEDT) postals are still arriving (until 4 Dec); many three-cornered contests may be uncounted on the right two-candidate pair, so "leading" may be undefined or reversed in several seats; ABC projections may be the only usable source.
- A deferred election in any seat (candidate death) reduces the seats available.

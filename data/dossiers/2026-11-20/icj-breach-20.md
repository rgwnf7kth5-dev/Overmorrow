# icj-breach-20
- **Claim:** In its judgment of Thursday 19 November 2026 in The Gambia v. Myanmar, the International Court of Justice finds that Myanmar breached at least one of its obligations under the Genocide Convention or the Court's provisional measures order of 23 January 2020.
- **Criterion:** YES if the operative paragraph (dispositif) of the merits judgment in case 178 (The Gambia v. Myanmar: 11 States intervening), delivered on 19 Nov 2026 (Europe/Amsterdam), finds that Myanmar has violated (breached) at least one obligation under the Genocide Convention — not to commit genocide, to prevent genocide, to punish genocide, or not to engage in conspiracy, incitement, attempt or complicity — or has failed to comply with the provisional measures indicated in the Order of 23 January 2020. Evidence: the judgment or ICJ press release on icj-cij.org, or Reuters, AP, AFP or BBC quoting the operative paragraph. NO if the Court rejects all of Gambia's claims, or if no judgment is delivered on 19 Nov 2026.

## Facts
- Gambia's submissions cover commission, conspiracy, failure to prevent, failure to punish, and non-compliance with the January 2020 provisional measures order; it also seeks cessation (genocide-specific criminal legislation, trials) and reparations — https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/11/03/questions-and-answers-international-court-of-justice-judgment-in-myanmar-genocide (published 2026-11-03T15:00-05:00, read 2026-11-17T17:53Z) "to find that Myanmar failed to comply with the provisional measures order of January 2020".
- The provisional measures order (Jan 2020) told Myanmar to prevent genocidal acts, ensure its forces did not commit them, preserve evidence, and report; Myanmar filed 12 reports (presidential directives, an investigative body, repatriation measures); Gambia says they give "no reason to conclude" compliance — HRW Q&A (same URL).
- Global Centre R2P: Gambia argued Myanmar's "blatant breach of the ICJ's provisional measures order ... and its active destruction of evidence" — https://www.globalr2p.org/publications/the-concluding-stage-of-the-gambia-v-myanmar-genocide-case-before-the-international-court-of-justice/ (published 2026-02-10T18:01Z, read 2026-11-17T17:53Z).
- Myanmar's defence: counter-terrorism against ARSA; no genocidal intent; UN Fact-Finding Mission reports should get no weight — HRW Q&A.
- Precedent Bosnia v. Serbia (2007): no attribution of genocide, but Serbia "breached its obligations to prevent genocide and to punish those responsible" — https://euobserver.com/248744/the-impact-of-the-icjs-genocide-ruling-in-myanmar-will-go-far-beyond-asia/ (published 2026-11-13T11:21+01:00, read 2026-11-17T17:52Z). (The duty to prevent arises only if genocide is committed by someone; in Croatia v. Serbia 2015 no genocide was found, so no breach followed — euobserver: "It rejected both Croatia's claim and Serbia's counterclaim".)
- Duty to punish: Gambia points to Myanmar's "failure to investigate gross violations" — HRW Q&A. No Myanmar prosecution for genocide is reported in the sources read.
- Jurisdiction and admissibility already settled (July 2022, 15–1) — globalr2p.
- See icj-genocide-20 for the standard-of-proof debate, bench questions and interveners.

## Base rate
- Class: ICJ merits judgments on Genocide Convention claims. Any breach found: 1/2 (Bosnia v. Serbia 2007 yes — prevent and punish; Croatia v. Serbia 2015 no). n=2, source euobserver. No reference-data entry applies.
- Note: a duty-to-prevent or duty-to-punish breach requires a finding that genocide occurred (by anyone); a provisional-measures breach does not.

## Risks
- If the Court finds no genocidal intent at all (as in Croatia v. Serbia), the prevent/punish claims fall with it; the only remaining route to YES is non-compliance with provisional measures, which the Court may treat as unproven or moot.
- A finding phrased only as "the Court takes note" or limited to reparations wording without a breach finding — NO.

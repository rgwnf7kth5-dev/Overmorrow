# eu-dbp-25
- **Claim:** In the European Semester autumn package adopted on Tuesday 24 November 2026, the European Commission concludes that France's 2027 draft budgetary plan complies with the Council's recommendations (it is not rated "at risk of non-compliance" or "not compliant").
- **Criterion:** YES if, between 20:00 on Sun 22 Nov and 23:59 on Tue 24 Nov 2026 (Europe/Brussels), the Commission adopts its opinion on France's 2027 Draft Budgetary Plan and that opinion's overall conclusion is that the plan "complies" / is "compliant" / is "in line" with the Council recommendation under the excessive deficit procedure (maximum net expenditure growth), as stated in the opinion itself (economy-finance.ec.europa.eu), the Commission's press release or communication on the 2027 draft budgetary plans, or reporting by AFP/Reuters/Euronews quoting it. Caveats in the opinion (e.g. "surrounded by considerable uncertainty", calls to adopt the budget) do not change a "complies" conclusion. NO if the conclusion is "at risk of non-compliance", "at risk of material non-compliance", "not compliant"/"not in line", if the Commission gives no conclusion on France (e.g. asks for an updated plan), or if the package is not adopted in the window (postponement). A report published after 23:59 about an opinion adopted in the window counts.
- **Event window:** 2026-11-22T20:00+01:00 – 2026-11-24T23:59+01:00 (Europe/Brussels); in practice the College meeting in Strasbourg on Tue 24 Nov morning.
- **Evidence expected:** documents on the Commission's economy-finance site and a press release around midday–early afternoon Tue 24 Nov (last year: 25 Nov 2025, same day); presentation in the EP plenary scrutiny debate Tue afternoon; AFP/Euronews/Le Monde same afternoon. evidence_at 2026-11-24T16:00+01:00.

## Facts
- **Last year's opinion on France (2026 DBP), 25 Nov 2025:** "Overall, the Commission is of the opinion that the Draft Budgetary Plan of France complies with the maximum growth of net expenditure in the Council Recommendation with a view to bringing an end to the situation of an excessive deficit. However, this assessment is surrounded by considerable uncertainty in view of the ongoing parliamentary discussions." — Commission opinion C(2025) 9057 https://economy-finance.ec.europa.eu/document/download/f29a0ce8-0b5c-460e-9f45-272b6af7907a_en?filename=C_2025_9057_1_EN_France.pdf (dated 25.11.2025, read 2026-11-22T17:54:45Z). The French PLF was then also still in parliament.
- **France's EDP path:** Council recommendation of 21 Jan 2025 — "net expenditure growth does not exceed 0.8% in 2025, 1.2% in 2026, 1.2% in 2027, 1.2% in 2028 and 1.1% in 2029… maximum cumulative growth rates calculated with reference to 2023 of 4.6% in 2025, 5.8% in 2026, 7.1% in 2027" — same opinion, recital 9. The excessive deficit is to be ended by 2029.
- **Last year's results for all countries (25 Nov 2025):** compliant: Cyprus, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Luxembourg, Portugal, Slovakia (12); at risk of non-compliance: Croatia, Lithuania, Slovenia; at risk of material non-compliance: Netherlands, Malta; Belgium, Spain and Austria were not assessed in the same way (no full DBP). Nine EDP countries, procedure steps held in abeyance until spring 2026 — ProtoThema English https://en.protothema.gr/2025/11/25/european-commission-gives-greece-a-positive-assessment-of-its-budget-nine-countries-in-excessive-deficit-procedure/ (published 2025-11-25T19:16Z, read 2026-11-22T17:55:04Z) "Twelve countries—including Greece, Cyprus, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Luxembourg, Portugal, and Slovakia—are deemed fully compliant".
- **France's 2027 draft budget:** presented 1 Oct 2026; deficit 5% of GDP in 2027 from 5.4% in 2026; about €54bn of savings; growth assumption 1%; the High Council of Public Finances called the scenario optimistic — EU Insider https://www.euinsider.eu/news/france-2027-budget-deficit-5-percent-watchdog-optimistic-2026 (published 2026-10-03, read 2026-11-22T17:54:35Z) "promising to shrink the deficit to 5% of GDP next year from a projected 5.4% this year".
- **Net expenditure track so far:** 2025 outturn 0.7% (cap 0.8%); the Commission's June 2026 forecast had 1.4% for 2026 (cap 1.2%) but cumulative 5.4% for 2024–26 against a 5.8% maximum — Toute l'Europe https://www.touteleurope.eu/economie-et-social/budget-2027-l-union-europeenne-peut-elle-imposer-des-reformes-a-la-france/ (published 2026-10-13T09:20Z, read 2026-11-22T17:55:13Z) "Selon les prévisions de juin, l'évolution cumulée des dépenses atteindrait cependant 5,4 % entre 2024 et 2026, contre un maximum autorisé de 5,8 %". France does not use the national escape clause for defence (same source: "Dix-huit États membres en bénéficient, mais pas la France").
- **Commission autumn 2026 forecast for France (17 Nov):** deficit 5.5% of GDP in 2026 (above the revised 5.4% aim), 5.4% in 2027, 5.7% in 2028 at unchanged policy; growth 0.4% (2026), 0.6% (2027); quoted: "In 2027, the general government deficit is expected to decline only slightly to 5.4% of GDP, as the projected muted economic growth and increasing interest payments on public debt offset the significant consolidation effort embedded in the forecast." — Briefs (summarising the Commission/Bloomberg report) https://www.briefs.co/news/european-commission-says-france-will-miss-deficit-targets/ (published 2026-11-17, read 2026-11-22T17:54:35Z). ## Risks
- The 2026 annual net expenditure overrun (June forecast 1.4% vs 1.2%), worsened by summer heatwave/fire costs, plus 2027 growth above 1.2% in the Commission's forecast could tip the cumulative check and give "at risk of non-compliance".
- The Assembly's rejection of the PLF: the Commission might judge the plan too uncertain to call compliant, or condition the assessment; a "complies… but uncertainty" wording still counts YES.
- A new label in the 2026 package wording ("not fully in line", "broadly compliant") would need reading; anything other than an unqualified "complies"/"compliant"/"in line" conclusion is NO under the criterion.

# Post-mortems

One paragraph for each confident miss: a claim at 70% or more that failed, or 30% or less that came true. Newest first.

## 2026-11-08 · ua-delegation-2 (14%, came true)

We gave 14% to Ukraine naming, on the record, the head of its delegation to the 10-11 November Abu Dhabi talks between 06:00 on 6 November and the end of 7 November; on 6 November Zelensky's communications adviser Dmytro Lytvyn ("No changes so far") and faction leader Arakhamia ("Umerov is head of negotiations") confirmed that Rustem Umerov leads it, in answer to media reports that the lead might change (Kyiv Independent). The forecasters treated the claim as a fresh, unscheduled announcement and started from the one-day announcement class (5 of 49), reading the January precedent of a decree on the day of the talks as the likely path. But Umerov was already the obvious lead, so any press question or rumour could draw an on-record confirmation, and the criterion counted a confirmation as naming. Only the outsider said so (30%). The research step was skipped because the search budget ran out, so the confusion story was never found. Diagnosis: bad claim design (the criterion could be met by a reaffirmation of a known fact, and "his office" had to cover an adviser's reply), with a missed fact and the wrong base rate as a result. Rule: before using the announcement class, ask whether the answer is already known. If it is, either ask about a formal act (a decree, an official list) or price the much higher chance of a casual confirmation.

## 2026-11-08 · py-vp-1 (3%, came true)

We gave 3% to Paraguay's Congress designating a new vice-president by the end of 7 November; on the night of 6 November, minutes after Alliana took the presidential oath, Congress president Basilio Núñez swore in Antonio Barrios as vice-president before the full Congress (Última Hora, ABC via MercoPress). The forecasters anchored on the 2012 (six days) and 2018 (about four weeks) designations and judged that the same-day plan had died with the abandoned resignation route. But the joint session was already sitting for the oath, Barrios had been put forward, and the governing Colorado faction that drove the removal could deliver the votes while it held the room. The 2012 and 2018 precedents came from different coalitions with no candidate agreed in advance. The calibration step also pulled a combined 6.9% down to 3%. Diagnosis: wrong base rate (old precedents were applied without asking whether the same session could do both steps), partly bad claim design: the criterion asked for designation "by an absolute majority", which no report stated, so the result is ambiguous. Rule: when a succession runs in one joint session, price each later step as conditional on that session continuing, not on precedents from separate sittings.

## 2026-11-08 · us-iran-strike-3 (5%, came true)

We gave 5% to a US-confirmed strike on Iranian territory on 5 or 6 November (Tehran time); Al Jazeera reported US projectiles hitting Qeshm Island on Friday night, 6 November, which Trump called "minor strikes", and Xinhua reported his statement that US forces had struck Iran's air defences again. The base-rate agent counted about one strike episode in 65 days. The others leaned on the Polymarket ceasefire contract (about 0.9 through 6 November) and on Trump saying there were "no immediate plans" after the midterms, and the calibration cut a combined 9.5% to 5%. But Qeshm had already been hit twice in the week before, so strikes were clustering, and Trump plays down strikes he goes on to order. The ceasefire market was about any breach and did not match our question. Diagnosis: wrong base rate (a long-run frequency was used during a cluster), with some bad luck. The timing against the 23:59 Tehran cut-off is uncertain, so the result is ambiguous. Rule: in an active strike cluster, use the frequency over the last one to two weeks, not the whole conflict, and do not let the calibration take a low-probability claim below the recent daily rate.

## 2026-11-06 · cz-chamber-any-1 (76%, failed)

We gave 76% to the Chamber of Deputies passing at least one of three government bills in third reading before its 34th sitting ended; the sitting closed on 6 November without a third-reading vote on any of them (ČT24, ČTK; psp.cz could not be read), after a power cut interrupted the Chamber and several items were postponed. The forecast treated a scheduled reading as the event and counted three chances as nearly independent, when all three hung on the same agenda running its course in the last days of a long sitting. Diagnosis: wrong base rate for agenda slippage at the end of a sitting, plus correlated outcomes treated as independent. Rule: when several claims depend on the same sitting reaching its items, apply the slip rate once to the whole set and model the items as correlated.

## 2026-10-31 · cz-fee-strike-rector-30 (26%, came true)

We gave 26% to a rector or the Rectors' Conference backing the 3 November strike for Czech Television and Czech Radio, or excusing students, within two days; on 30 October Masaryk University's leadership decided to excuse students who attend the rally (Deník N, Brněnský deník). The forecasters started from our own class of unscheduled announcements within a day (then 1 in 20) and treated the criterion as one decision, when in fact some twenty-six public universities could each meet it and the academic community had been mobilising openly for a week. Diagnosis: wrong base rate (a one-actor class used for a claim that any of many actors could satisfy). Rule: when a criterion is met by any one of N independent actors, estimate per actor and combine, 1 − (1 − p)^N, instead of using a single-announcement base rate.

## 2026-10-29 · cz-pavel-speech-media-28 (70%, failed)

We gave 70% to President Pavel naming Czech Television, Czech Radio, public-service media or the licence fee in his 28 October speech; he spoke only of trust in "institutions, the media and science" and of not breaking up "the media space" by ill-considered changes to the law (Forum24 full text, ČT24). The forecast read the political context correctly — the speech did address the fee bill — but the claim required an explicit name, and Pavel's habit in set-piece speeches is to allude rather than name what is before Parliament. Diagnosis: bad claim design (a wording test where the substance was the real question) together with a wrong base rate for explicit naming. Rule: for speech claims, base the probability on the speaker's past wording in comparable speeches, and if the substance is what matters, write the criterion on the substance (for example "criticises the bill on public-service media funding").

## 2026-10-27 · br-loser-concedes (75%, failed)

We said the loser of Brazil's runoff would publicly acknowledge defeat or congratulate the winner in time; Flávio Bolsonaro said on election night that it was "impossible to recognise the result" and repeated it in a video on 26 October, while only his party, the PL, recognised the TSE's result. The forecast leaned on the general norm of concession in Latin American runoffs and on the PL's institutional behaviour, but the candidate himself had spent the campaign casting doubt on the voting extension and the family's 2022 precedent was a refusal to concede. Diagnosis: wrong base rate (a generic concession norm applied to a candidate with a stated record of contesting results). Rule: for concession claims, start from the candidate's own public record and statements in the final week, not the regional norm.

## 2026-10-26 · br-turnout-2022 (22%, came true)

We gave only 22% to turnout in the runoff exceeding the 79.41% of 2022; the TSE reported about 80.8%. The forecast treated 2022 as an unusually high peak and assumed mean reversion, but a close, polarised rematch-style runoff with a high-salience incumbent drew the same mobilisation, and the two-hour voting extension in three states added to it. Diagnosis: wrong base rate (mean reversion applied where the drivers of the previous peak were still present). Rule: for turnout claims against the last comparable election, condition on closeness and polarisation of the race, not only on the long-run average. (The same edition's Lula vote-share range, 47.3–50.9%, also missed the result of 51.79%, and br-flavio-wins at 69% failed: the paper read the late Datafolha lead as more solid than the house's own series supported.)

## 2026-10-27 · cz-pardubice-coalition-signed-26 (27%, came true)

We gave 27% to the five Pardubice groupings signing their coalition agreement on 26 October; they signed it that evening. The forecast started from the negotiation-round slip rate and from the earlier miss of a signing by noon, reading the delay as a sign of trouble, when the parties had publicly set the signing for that day. Diagnosis: missed fact compounded by over-weighting the slip prior: an announced signing date is a scheduled event, not an open negotiation. Rule: when partners announce the date of a signing, use the scheduled-event class, not negotiation-round.

## 2026-10-26 · sk-vallo-10 (35%, came true)

We gave Vallo only a 35% chance of beating Ráž by more than 10 points; ŠÚ SR results gave him 64.36% to 32.32%, a 32-point margin. The forecast ran the national poll-lead model on a single local poll showing a 7-point lead, and its own base-rate note already said the pooled error "understates spread for one local n=500 poll" — yet the spread was used to cap the margin rather than widen it. Incumbent big-city mayors with a fragmented or weak opposition tend to win by landslides that one small poll does not capture. Diagnosis: wrong base rate (a national-election error model applied to a municipal race, anchored on one poll). Rule: for local races with one poll, take the incumbent's previous margin as the anchor and widen the error well beyond the pooled rms.

## 2026-10-25 · br-datafolha-flavio (28%, came true)

We said Datafolha's 23–24 October poll would not show Flávio Bolsonaro strictly ahead; it gave him 51% to Lula's 49% of valid votes. Datafolha's own runoff series had shown Flávio at 52–48 (6–7 Oct) and 51–49 (13–15 Oct) before a 50–50 tie on 22 Oct, so "strictly ahead" was close to a coin flip on the house's own trend, not a 28% outcome. Diagnosis: wrong base rate from over-reading the last tie as a trend toward Lula. (Lula went on to win the actual runoff, so the poll itself was off; the claim was about the poll.) Rule: for claims about a named pollster's next release, start from the distribution of that house's last three rounds.


## 2026-10-23 · israel-no-majority (80%, failed)

We said no poll dated 22 October would give either Israeli bloc 61 seats; Channel 14's final pre-blackout poll (Direct Polls/Filber) gave the 37th-government parties 62, and Wikipedia's table highlights it as a majority. Channel 14 had already shown the government bloc at 61 in earlier October polls listed in the same table, and it publishes almost daily, so the claim effectively asked whether its 22 October poll would fall below 61. Diagnosis: wrong base rate, from a missed fact: the forecast looked at the average of pollsters, where neither bloc reaches 61, instead of at the most favourable house, which is the one that decides a "no poll gives either bloc" claim. Rule: for "any poll" claims, set the probability from the outlier pollster's recent figures, not from the polling average.

## 2026-10-24 · cz-benefits-stalled (75%, failed)

We said the unemployment-benefit cut would stay stuck in the Chamber on 23 October; in fact the government had attached the cut to the Labour Code amendment (tisk 300), which passed its first reading on 20 October (motion to reject failed 43–78). The story rested on a ČT24 article of 8 October and nobody re-checked the Chamber's own record before freezing. Diagnosis: missed fact, compounded by bad claim design — the criterion spoke of a "benefit bill" that did not exist as a separate print, so the resolver had to interpret it. Rule: Chamber claims must name the print number (tisk) and be checked against psp.cz on the morning of the freeze.

## 2026-10-23 · sakharov-icc (15%, came true)

The Sakharov Prize went to the International Criminal Court and its officials, the finalist we rated least likely. We leaned on the Conference of Presidents arithmetic, assuming the EPP and ECR would carry Lissu, and gave too little weight to the chance that group leaders would converge on a laureate that defended international institutions under pressure. Diagnosis: wrong base rate. The class "laureate is the EPP-backed finalist" was untested and built from a handful of recent years; with this result it stands at 0 of 1 in our own record, and future Sakharov forecasts should start closer to an even split among finalists until the record grows. The pilot edition also lacked the three-agent ensemble that might have challenged the assumption.
